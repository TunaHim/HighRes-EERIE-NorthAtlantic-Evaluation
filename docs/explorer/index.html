About this index

About the three runs

Each coupled model has three historical runs (r1, r2, r3) with the same forcing but different initial conditions. Select an individual run to examine its climate realisation, or the mean of all three to reduce the influence of run-specific variability. Features consistent across all three sampled runs provide evidence of a common model response; differences between runs illustrate the influence of internal variability. Three runs, however, cannot fully quantify the model's internal variability or guarantee that the same features occur in every realisation. The runs are compared in notebook 10 and the control runs in notebook 11; HadGEM3-GC5E is not included here because only its monthly data are public (see notebook 12).

What the indices and options mean

Wettest day (RX1day): the largest one-day rainfall in the year or winter. Rain on very wet days (R95p): total rain on days wetter than the local 95th percentile of wet days (wet day: at least 1 mm). Share of rain on very wet days: R95p as a percentage of all wet-day rain. Hottest day (TXx): the highest daily maximum temperature.

Difference from a reference: percent for the rainfall indices, °C for TXx. Orange means the dataset is wetter or warmer than the reference, blue drier or cooler. Correlation with the winter NAO: how consistently the winter index rises in positive-NAO winters (29 winters); each model run uses its own NAO, E-OBS and ERA5 the ERA5 NAO. Two different calculations: the correlation maps use linearly detrended winter series; the scatter plot uses the raw, non-detrended regional values, with the correlation shown separately for each run. On the maps, values beyond about ±0.38 pass a nominal, unadjusted 5% test at a single grid cell; this allows neither for autocorrelation nor for testing many grid cells at once. The full uncertainty analysis (bootstrap intervals, false-discovery-rate control) is in notebook 06 and, for the three runs, notebook 10.

Regional averages use the same land cells for every dataset (land where E-OBS has data).

Spread across r1–r3: at each grid cell, the largest minus the smallest of the three runs' period averages (for the selected model, index and period). Where it is small compared with the model's difference from the references, the difference is consistent across the sampled runs; where it is large, the feature depends on run-specific variability.

Model runs: r1, r2 and r3 share the forcing and differ only in their starting state, so their differences illustrate the influence of internal variability over 29 winters; three runs cannot quantify it fully (the control runs in notebook 11 give a longer sample). Mean of r1–r3 averages the three maps (for the NAO view, the three correlation maps; each run is correlated with its own NAO). Averaging reduces the influence of run-specific variability, but the year-to-year line of the mean is smoother than any single run or the observations, and the scatter plot then shows the three runs separately rather than one pooled relationship. The NAO of runs r2 and r3 is measured on the ERA5 pattern with anomalies relative to 1975–2014 (they start in 1975).

Maps are averaged to 0.5° for this page; the notebooks use the full 0.25° grid. Data and methods: see the notebooks and the methods page. The provenance of the data behind this page (code version, inputs, date) is stored in data.json.

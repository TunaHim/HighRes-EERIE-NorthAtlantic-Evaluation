10 · Model or chance? Three runs of each coupled model¶

Question for this notebook. Notebooks 03–09 evaluate one run (member r1) of each coupled model. A single run carries its own chance weather, so every model result in notebooks 04–06 could be a property of the model or a lucky or unlucky run. The EERIE archive holds two more historical runs of each model (r2 and r3). They use the same model and the same external forcing and differ only in tiny changes to the starting state, so after a few years their weather is independent. This notebook repeats their key diagnostics on all three runs and asks, result by result: do all three runs agree?

Data.

  • ICON-ESM-ER and IFS-FESOM2-SR, historical runs r1, r2 and r3. The r2 and r3 runs start in 1975, so the NAO is compared over the common 39 winters 1976–2014; extremes over the 29 winters 1986–2014, as in notebooks 05 and 06.
  • References as before: ERA5 (pressure, rain, temperature) and E-OBS (rain and temperature over land).
  • IFS-FESOM2-SR r3 has no data for 2–31 October 1999 on the server. October is not part of winter, so the winter results are unaffected; the annual indices for 1999 miss those days.

How to read the results. Three runs are still a small ensemble: their range shows how much chance alone can move a number, but it is not a full uncertainty interval. A result that holds in all three runs is called consistent across the three sampled runs; one that changes sign or size strongly between runs is sensitive to chance. Notebook 11 adds the long control runs to measure chance more completely.

Background: the terms used in this notebook¶

Term What it means here
Member (run) r1, r2, r3 Three simulations of the same model with the same forcing, started from slightly different states. Their differences are internal variability: chance weather.
Consistent across the three sampled runs All three runs give the same sign and a similar size. With only three runs this is necessary but not sufficient for a property of the model.
NAO strength Standard deviation of the winter pressure index measured along the common ERA5 NAO pattern, divided by the same quantity for ERA5 over the same winters (notebook 04).
Mean circulation offset The model's average winter pressure minus ERA5's, projected onto the ERA5 NAO pattern, in ERA5 standard deviations (notebook 04, section 7).
NAO link Detrended correlation between the winter NAO index and a winter extreme index, land average of a region (notebook 06). Each run uses its own NAO; E-OBS and ERA5 use the ERA5 NAO.
Bootstrap 95% interval The range of the correlation when the winters are resampled 5000 times (notebook 06, section 10).

Colours. Dark grey ERA5, light grey E-OBS, blue ICON-ESM-ER (all runs), orange IFS-FESOM2-SR (all runs).

What this means, in plain words

  1. The large biases are consistent across the three sampled runs, not bad luck. ICON-ESM-ER's shifted mean circulation (about +2 SD along the NAO), its 50–60% excess in very-wet-day rain over UK and Ireland, its too many and too heavy wet days and its 3 °C too-cool hottest day appear in all three runs with similar size. The same holds for IFS-FESOM2-SR's much smaller biases. The single-run diagnosis of the mean state and of extremes in notebooks 03–05 stands.
  2. A weak NAO is a property of both models. All six runs have an NAO weaker than ERA5's over the same winters: ICON-ESM-ER 0.55–0.63, IFS-FESOM2-SR 0.68–0.81. The ranking between the models is also the same in every run.
  3. ICON-ESM-ER's "mixed" leading pattern was mostly r1's chance. In r2 and r3 its own leading pattern resembles ERA5's well (pattern correlation 0.90 and 0.94, against 0.59 in r1 over these winters), although its first two patterns remain close in variance. The emphasis on the mixing in notebook 04 should be softened; measuring every run along the common ERA5 pattern was the right safeguard.
  4. The NAO link over UK and Ireland cannot be pinned down with single runs. Three runs of one model span 0.06 to 0.49 (ICON-ESM-ER) and 0.21 to 0.60 (IFS-FESOM2-SR): as wide as the difference between the two references. Notebook 06's statement that IFS-FESOM2-SR reproduces the UK link better than ICON-ESM-ER does not hold; both are consistent with the observations and with each other.
  5. Over western Norway both models are consistently too weak. All six runs have a weaker NAO link (0.52–0.71) than both references (0.81, bootstrap 95% interval about 0.71–0.91). This is the clearest model–observation difference in the NAO-to-extremes link, and it was underplayed in notebook 06; notebook 11 tests it against the control runs.
  6. The wrong-sign link over north-west France appears in two of three runs, partly chance. ICON-ESM-ER is negative in two of three runs (−0.46, −0.32) and weakly positive in the third (+0.18); IFS-FESOM2-SR is near zero in two runs. Notebook 11 tests this against the control runs.
  7. The circulation share of ICON-ESM-ER's wet bias is stable across runs (74–82% of the R95p bias against E-OBS, 38–41% against ERA5), so the decomposition in notebook 06 was not a single-run artefact.

Answer to the question. Run r1's results on the mean circulation, NAO strength, rainfall and temperature extremes and the wet-day statistics are consistent across the three sampled runs. Its results on the NAO-to-extremes link only partly hold: over UK and Ireland the link varies between runs as much as between models and references, over western Norway both models are consistently too weak in all three runs (a modest discrepancy, given the observed value's uncertainty), and ICON-ESM-ER's wrong sign over north-west France appears in two of three runs. ICON-ESM-ER's mixed leading NAO pattern was largely the chance of run r1.

10. Limitations¶

  • Three runs are few. Their range shows that chance can move a result; it does not give a full probability distribution. A result that agrees in three runs can still differ in a fourth. Notebook 11 uses the 100-year control runs for a fuller estimate of chance.
  • The r2 and r3 runs start in 1975, so the NAO window here (39 winters) is shorter than in notebook 04 (64 winters); r1's numbers differ slightly from notebook 04 for that reason alone.
  • IFS-FESOM2-SR r3 misses 2–31 October 1999 (server data gap); winter results are unaffected.
  • All other limitations of notebooks 04–06 apply (references, regridding, linear methods).

11. Open questions¶

  1. Is the spread between three runs representative of chance, or are the extra runs systematically different (for example because they start from a different ocean state)? Notebook 11 compares it with the control runs.
  2. With ten or more runs per model, would the NAO link over UK and Ireland settle on one value, and would it match the observations?

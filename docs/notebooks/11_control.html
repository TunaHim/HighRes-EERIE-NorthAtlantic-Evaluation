11 · How much can chance alone do? 100 years of control runs¶

Question for this notebook. Notebook 10 showed that three runs of one model can disagree on the NAO link to rain extremes. But how wide is the range that chance alone produces? The EERIE archive has a control run of each coupled model: 100 years (1950–2049) with the external forcing held at 1950 conditions, so every difference between one stretch of years and another is internal variability. Cutting it into windows of 29 winters, the length used for extremes in version 1, gives the chance range of each diagnostic for that model. A historical run or an observed value outside that range is unlikely to be chance.

Data.

  • ICON-ESM-ER and IFS-FESOM2-SR control runs (eerie-control-1950): monthly sea-level pressure, 1950–2049 (99 complete winters), and daily precipitation over north-west Europe.
  • For comparison: ERA5 and E-OBS (notebook 06) and the three historical runs (notebook 10).

Method.

  1. NAO index of each control winter along the ERA5 pattern, in ERA5 standard deviations (anomalies relative to the control run's own 100-year winter mean).
  2. Winter RX1day and R95p from the control run's daily rain (the very-wet-day threshold from all 100 control winters), land average of each region, same land cells as before.
  3. Every window of 29 consecutive winters (71 windows, starting 1951 to 2021): NAO strength and the detrended correlation between the NAO and each regional rain index.
  4. The 5th to 95th percentile of those 71 values is the chance range.

A caution. Consecutive windows overlap, so the 71 values are not independent: 99 winters hold only about three independent 29-winter samples. The range is therefore an estimate, likely a little too narrow in the tails. It is still far better than the spread of three runs.

Background: the terms used in this notebook¶

Term What it means here
Control run A simulation with constant 1950 greenhouse gases, aerosols and other forcing. It has weather and year-to-year variability but no climate change, so it shows the model's internal variability alone.
Window 29 consecutive winters of the control run, the same length as the 1986–2014 period of version 1.
Chance range The 5th to 95th percentile of a diagnostic over all windows: values that the model produces by chance in 90% of 29-winter periods.
Inside / outside the chance range A historical run inside the range is consistent with chance; an observed value outside it means the model rarely produces what was observed.
Drift A slow trend in a control run as the model adjusts. Series are detrended within each window, as in version 1.

What this means, in plain words

  1. No 29-winter period of either model reaches the observed NAO strength. ERA5's NAO over 1986–2014 has a strength of 1.04 in these units; the strongest window of ICON-ESM-ER's control run reaches 0.80, of IFS-FESOM2-SR's 0.95. Together with notebook 10 (all six historical runs weaker than ERA5), a too-weak NAO is a robust property of both models, more pronounced in ICON-ESM-ER.
  2. Over western Norway the observed NAO link is stronger than in all sampled model periods, but not by much. The observed correlation (0.81) lies above every one of the 71 control windows of both models (highest 0.77 and 0.78) and above all six historical runs (0.52–0.71). The observed value has its own sampling uncertainty, however: its bootstrap 95% interval is 0.71–0.91 (E-OBS) and 0.76–0.89 (ERA5), which overlaps the models' strongest windows. This is a consistent discrepancy within the available simulations, the clearest in the NAO-to-extremes link, but the small number of independent control windows and the uncertainty of the observed value limit how strongly it can be stated.
  3. Over north-west France ICON-ESM-ER's link has the wrong sign as a model property. Its control run almost never produces a positive link (highest window 0.11, median −0.14), while both references show about +0.3. The third historical run's +0.18 is the exception, not the rule. IFS-FESOM2-SR's chance range reaches up to the observed values, so it is consistent with them.
  4. Over UK and Ireland the observations are within chance for IFS-FESOM2-SR, and at the edge for ICON-ESM-ER. ERA5's 0.35 lies inside both chance ranges; E-OBS's 0.51 lies inside IFS-FESOM2-SR's range but above ICON-ESM-ER's (0.26 to 0.40). With the two references disagreeing, no firm verdict is possible.
  5. The control runs underestimate chance. Several historical runs fall outside their model's control range (for example ICON-ESM-ER r2 at 0.06 for UK and Ireland, below its control range of 0.26 to 0.40). Overlapping windows from one 99-winter run sample only about three independent periods, and the historical runs also differ in forcing. The ranges are therefore lower bounds on chance; differences that lie well outside them, as for the NAO strength and ICON-ESM-ER's link over north-west France, are the ones that can be trusted.
  6. No drift, but the ranges depend on which years are sampled (section 7). Trends are negligible (NAO within ±0.04 SD per decade, UK R95p within ±0.2% per decade), and about half of the median window statistics of the first and second 50 years agree within 0.05 (for example ICON-ESM-ER over western Norway 0.63 and 0.67, over UK and Ireland 0.35 and 0.33). Others differ by up to about 0.2 (IFS-FESOM2-SR over western Norway 0.51 and 0.69; ICON-ESM-ER RX1day over north-west France 0.26 and 0.07), as expected when 50 years hold only one or two independent 29-winter samples, so the chance ranges themselves are uncertain. NAO strength is stable in ICON-ESM-ER (median 0.72 and 0.71) but not in IFS-FESOM2-SR (0.60 in the first half, 0.85 in the second): slow multidecadal variability or a small adjustment of the model, which widens its chance range.

Answer to the question. Chance alone moves the NAO link to UK and Ireland rain extremes by about ±0.1 to ±0.2 within one model, enough to cover the difference between the two models and between the two references. It does not explain three results within the available simulations: both models' NAO is too weak, both models' NAO link over western Norway is weaker than observed (a consistent but modest discrepancy, given the observed value's own uncertainty), and ICON-ESM-ER's link over north-west France has the wrong sign.

4. NAO strength: chance range¶

What this cell does. For every 29-winter window of each control run, the NAO strength (standard deviation of the index along the ERA5 pattern, in ERA5 units). Then the chance range, and for comparison the strength of the three historical runs over 1986–2014 in the same units.

In [4]:
def windows(series):
    years = series.year.values
    return [np.arange(start, start + WINDOW) for start in range(years[0], years[-1] - WINDOW + 2)]

historical = {m: [m, f'{m}.r2', f'{m}.r3'] for m in CONTROLS}

def historical_nao(run):
    psl = store.load(run, 'mon', 'psl', 'north_atlantic')
    w = nao.winter_anomalies(clim.period(psl, 1975, 2014), (1975, 2014))
    return (nao.project(w, era5_nao.vector) / era5_nao.amplitude).sel(year=slice(1986, 2014))

era5_window = (nao.project(nao.winter_anomalies(store.load('era5', 'mon', 'psl', 'north_atlantic')), era5_nao.vector)
               / era5_nao.amplitude).sel(year=slice(1986, 2014))
rows = []
for m in CONTROLS:
    values = [float(control_nao[m].sel(year=y).std(ddof=1)) for y in windows(control_nao[m])]
    runs = [float(historical_nao(r).std(ddof=1)) for r in historical[m]]
    rows.append({'model': LABEL(m).split()[0], 'windows': len(values), 'chance range 5%': np.percentile(values, 5),
                 'median': np.median(values), 'chance range 95%': np.percentile(values, 95), 'highest window': max(values),
                 'historical runs 1986-2014': ', '.join(f'{v:.2f}' for v in runs)})
strength = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index('model')
print(f'ERA5 1986-2014: {float(era5_window.std(ddof=1)):.2f}')

7. Is the control run stable? First against second half¶

A control run is useful for chance ranges only if its statistics do not drift. This section compares windows that lie entirely in the first half of the run (1951–1999, window starts up to 1971) with windows entirely in the second half (2000–2049). Similar medians mean no drift; the difference between the halves also shows how much the range itself depends on which 50 years are sampled.

What this cell does. For NAO strength and for each region and rain index: the median and the range over the windows of each half of each control run.

9. Limitations¶

  • Overlapping windows. 99 winters contain only about three independent 29-winter samples, so the chance range is an estimate and probably slightly too narrow at the tails.
  • 1950 forcing. The control runs have no climate change; the historical runs do. Where forcing itself changes the NAO link, the comparison mixes the two. Within 1986–2014 this effect is expected to be small compared with internal variability.
  • Model chance, not observational chance. The range shows what this model does by chance; it is used to ask whether the observed value is unusual for the model.
  • Daily rain only. The control runs were downloaded with daily precipitation only, so TXx is not tested here.

10. Open questions¶

  1. Is the observed link over western Norway (0.81) outside the chance range of both models because of their weak NAO, or because of how rain responds to a given NAO swing?
  2. Would a longer control run, or several, widen the ranges enough to include the observations?

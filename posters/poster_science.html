Climate-model evaluation requires separating persistent model biases from internal variability and resolution sensitivity. A better large-scale circulation does not necessarily produce more realistic regional rainfall.

Research question

Which features of the simulated North Atlantic winter circulation and of European winter extremes are persistent model characteristics, which arise from internal variability, and how do they change across coupled model resolution configurations?

Models of the EU project EERIE against ERA5 (reanalysis) and E-OBS (gridded daily data based on station observations) over north-west Europe; UK and Ireland, western Norway and north-west France as worked examples.

Three experiments, three questions

Each experiment uses the data that can answer its question; all share the same diagnostics and references.

Experiment 1 · historical ensembleSix runs: which biases persist?

Winter NAO–R95p correlation, 1986–2014, with bootstrap 95% intervals: E-OBS, ERA5, then three runs each of ICON-ESM-ER (blue) and IFS-FESOM2-SR (orange).

ICON-ESM-ER's shifted circulation (+1.9 to +2.1 SD along the NAO) and its 50–62% excess of very-wet-day rain over UK and Ireland appear in all three runs; the circulation is consistent with only part of that wet bias. Both models' NAO is too weak in every run (55–63% and 68–81% of ERA5). The NAO link over UK and Ireland varies between runs as much as between models (0.06–0.49 and 0.21–0.60).

Experiment 2 · internal variabilityWhat can chance do in 29 winters?

Control runs with constant 1950 forcing: 99 complete winters, 71 overlapping 29-winter windows; the empirical 5th–95th percentile across windows is the chance range (below: shaded).

Across the sampled control windows, internal variability shifts the UK and Ireland NAO–R95p correlation by about ±0.1 to ±0.2. No control window reaches the observed NAO strength. Over western Norway, simulated correlations remain below the observed point estimate (0.81), although the strongest model values reach its 95% interval (0.71–0.91). ICON-ESM-ER almost never produces the observed link over north-west France.

Experiment 3 · resolutionDoes higher resolution help?

HadGEM3-GC5E coupled atmosphere–ocean configurations (LL/MM/HH); atmospheric and ocean resolution increase together. One run per configuration, monthly data.

Pressure error falls (3.2 → 2.7 → 2.6 hPa) and the NAO pattern correlation increases, but NAO strength does not follow resolution and winter-mean rain over UK and Ireland rises to 25% above ERA5 at 20 km. In IFS (same SSTs), 9 km instead of 28 km makes winter rain extremes 5–12% heavier.

Conclusions

  1. Persistent biases such as ICON-ESM-ER's circulation and wet bias are consistent across the sampled runs.
  2. Relationships such as the NAO–rain link depend strongly on chance in 29 winters: one run cannot rank models.
  3. Resolution improves the large-scale circulation, not automatically the regional rainfall.

Limitations

Three historical runs per model. The 71 control-run windows overlap and are not independent; their empirical range should not be interpreted as a formal probability interval and may underrepresent the full variability. HadGEM3-GC5E with one run per configuration, monthly data only, atmosphere and ocean refined together; 29 winters for the link to extremes. Exploratory results, not a ranking of models.

**What this means, in plain words**

1. **No 29-winter period of either model reaches the observed NAO strength.** ERA5's NAO over 1986–2014 has a strength of 1.04 in these units; the strongest window of ICON-ESM-ER's control run reaches 0.80, of IFS-FESOM2-SR's 0.95. Together with notebook 10 (all six historical runs weaker than ERA5), a too-weak NAO is consistent across both models' runs and control periods, more pronounced in ICON-ESM-ER.
2. **Over western Norway the observed NAO link is stronger than in all sampled model periods, but not by much.** The observed correlation (0.81) lies above every one of the 71 control windows of both models (highest 0.77 and 0.78) and above all six historical runs (0.52–0.71). The observed value has its own sampling uncertainty, however: its bootstrap 95% interval is 0.71–0.91 (E-OBS) and 0.76–0.89 (ERA5), which overlaps the models' strongest windows. This is a consistent discrepancy within the available simulations, the clearest in the NAO-to-extremes link, but the small number of independent control windows and the uncertainty of the observed value limit how strongly it can be stated.
3. **Over north-west France ICON-ESM-ER's link has the wrong sign in almost every control period.** Its control run almost never produces a positive link (highest window 0.11, median −0.14), while both references show about +0.3. The third historical run's +0.18 is the exception, not the rule. IFS-FESOM2-SR's chance range reaches up to the observed values, so it is consistent with them.
4. **Over UK and Ireland the observations are within chance for IFS-FESOM2-SR, and at the edge for ICON-ESM-ER.** ERA5's 0.35 lies inside both chance ranges; E-OBS's 0.51 lies inside IFS-FESOM2-SR's range but above ICON-ESM-ER's (0.26 to 0.40). With the two references disagreeing, no firm verdict is possible.
5. **The control runs underestimate chance.** Several historical runs fall outside their model's control range (for example ICON-ESM-ER r2 at 0.06 for UK and Ireland, below its control range of 0.26 to 0.40). Overlapping windows from one 99-winter run sample only about three independent periods, and the historical runs also differ in forcing. The ranges are therefore lower bounds on chance; differences that lie well outside them, as for the NAO strength and ICON-ESM-ER's link over north-west France, are the ones that can be trusted.
6. **No drift, but the ranges depend on which years are sampled** (section 7). Trends are negligible (NAO within ±0.04 SD per decade, UK R95p within ±0.2% per decade), and about half of the median window statistics of the first and second 50 years agree within 0.05 (for example ICON-ESM-ER over western Norway 0.63 and 0.67, over UK and Ireland 0.35 and 0.33). Others differ by up to about 0.2 (IFS-FESOM2-SR over western Norway 0.51 and 0.69; ICON-ESM-ER RX1day over north-west France 0.26 and 0.07), as expected when 50 years hold only one or two independent 29-winter samples, so the chance ranges themselves are uncertain. NAO strength is stable in ICON-ESM-ER (median 0.72 and 0.71) but not in IFS-FESOM2-SR (0.60 in the first half, 0.85 in the second): slow multidecadal variability or a small adjustment of the model, which widens its chance range.

**Answer to the question.** Chance alone moves the NAO link to UK and Ireland rain extremes by about ±0.1 to ±0.2 within one model, enough to cover the difference between the two models and between the two references. It does not explain three results within the available simulations: both models' NAO is too weak, both models' NAO link over western Norway is weaker than observed (a consistent but modest discrepancy, given the observed value's own uncertainty), and ICON-ESM-ER's link over north-west France has the wrong sign.

**What this means, in plain words**

1. **Higher resolution makes winter rain heavier.** At 9 km the wettest winter day and the rain on very wet days are 5–12% larger than at 28 km, and in most places the difference lies outside the range of the three 28 km members. The increase is largest over mountains and west-facing coasts (western Norway, western Scotland, the Alps), where finer terrain lifts moist air more sharply.
2. **Heavier is not automatically better.** Over western Norway, where 28 km falls short of both references, 9 km comes closer to them. Over UK and Ireland, where 28 km is already about right, 9 km overshoots. Across the whole region the 9 km maps have a slightly *larger* typical error than any 28 km member against both references, while the pattern is about equally good (pattern correlations 0.81–0.94 for all runs). At the 25 km scale compared here, running at 9 km brings no overall gain for winter rainfall extremes in this model, and a local gain over steep terrain.
3. **The conclusion about drivers does not depend on resolution, but it does depend on chance.** The NAO-to-extremes correlation at 9 km lies within, or close to, the range of the three 28 km members in every region. That range is itself wide: from 0.04 to 0.33 over UK and Ireland, and from −0.38 to +0.28 over north-west France, in runs of the *same* model with the *same* ocean. With 29 winters, internal variability alone can change the answer to "how strongly does the NAO drive UK rainfall extremes in this model" more than going from 28 km to 9 km does.
4. **The strong link over western Norway is robust to both:** 0.63 to 0.75 in every run, close to both references (0.81).

**Answer to the question.** In this model, going from 28 km to 9 km makes winter rainfall extremes 5–12% heavier, helps over steep terrain such as western Norway, and does not improve the region as a whole at the 25 km scale. The qualitative conclusion about the NAO's influence does not change with resolution; it is far more sensitive to internal variability, which a single run cannot capture. These are exploratory results from one 9 km run and three 28 km members, for winter only.

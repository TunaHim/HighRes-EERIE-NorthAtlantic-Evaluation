**What this means, in plain words**

1. **The data are complete:** 30 years of daily files for both models and both references, 29 complete winters.
2. **The two references agree closely with each other.** ERA5's rainfall extremes are a few percent lower than E-OBS's and its hottest days about 1.4 °C cooler. That spread is the uncertainty in "the observed value" that any model comparison has to live with.
3. **IFS-FESOM2-SR's rainfall extremes are close to both references:** within about 10% of E-OBS and 15–20% of ERA5 over UK and Ireland, and within a few percent in north-west France.
4. **ICON-ESM-ER's rainfall extremes are far too heavy against either reference:** about +50% to +60% for the year and up to +80% in winter, in all three regions. This continues the chain seen earlier: too-strong westerlies (notebook 03), a mean state like a permanent positive NAO (notebook 04), too much winter rain on average, and now too-heavy extremes. The excess also appears in north-west France and in summer-dominated annual maxima, so the circulation bias is probably not the whole story.
5. **The share of rain on very wet days is similar in all four datasets** (18–20%): ICON-ESM-ER rains too much on ordinary and extreme days alike.
6. **Hottest days: with ERA5 the picture becomes clearer.** Against E-OBS both models looked too cool by 2–3 °C. Against ERA5, which is on a grid like the models', IFS-FESOM2-SR is only 0.6 °C cooler, so most of its apparent deficit was the comparison of grid cells with low-lying stations, not a model fault. ICON-ESM-ER remains about 2 °C too cool against ERA5, so its hot extremes are genuinely too mild.
7. **Year-to-year spread** of the regional extremes is of the right order in both models. ICON-ESM-ER's hottest day varies less from year to year (about 1.0 °C against 1.6 °C in both references), in line with its low temperature variability in notebook 03.

**Bottom line.** IFS-FESOM2-SR's rainfall and heat extremes are credible against both references. ICON-ESM-ER's rainfall extremes are clearly too heavy and its hottest days too mild. These are exploratory results from one member each.
